Solar Marketing in Noida: How Installers Can Win RWA and Factory Rooftop Projects

A solar marketing agency Noida playbook for installers chasing housing society (RWA) and factory rooftop projects across Noida, Greater Noida West and the industrial sectors.

By Kiran Growth School team·15 November 2026·6 min read
Quick answer

Noida installers win RWA and factory projects by running two separate funnels. Societies need a committee-ready proposal built around the PM Surya Ghar GHS/RWA assistance of ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities, while factories need a payback and bill-savings case, because commercial and industrial rooftops are outside PM Surya Ghar. Kiran Growth School builds both funnels with Google Search, Meta Ads, separate landing pages and WhatsApp follow-up.

Noida has two rooftop markets sitting side by side. On one side are hundreds of group housing societies in Sector 137, Sector 75, Sector 150 and Greater Noida West. On the other are factories and warehouses in Phase 1, Phase 2, Hosiery Complex, Sector 63 and the Ecotech belts of Greater Noida. Both have big roofs and big bills. Both buy very differently from a homeowner.

This guide is for installers and EPC companies looking for a solar marketing agency Noida approach that wins these larger projects instead of only chasing 3 kW home leads.

Why RWAs and factories are worth the extra effort

A single society or factory project can equal ten or twenty home installations in capacity. The sales cycle is longer, but the competition is thinner, because most installer marketing in Noida is aimed at homeowners and the PM Surya Ghar ₹78,000 subsidy.

The policy backdrop is also helpful. As of November 2026:

  • PM Surya Ghar has reached about 66 lakh households nationally, according to the Union Minister for New and Renewable Energy, so committees and owners already know the scheme exists.
  • Group housing societies and RWAs can get central assistance of ₹18,000 per kW for common facilities, including EV charging, up to 500 kW (calculated at 3 kW per house, and including residents' own rooftop plants).
  • Uttar Pradesh has offered a residential state top-up of ₹15,000 per kW, capped at ₹30,000. This is for homes, not common areas or factories.
  • Commercial and industrial (C&I) rooftops are not covered by PM Surya Ghar. They are sold on savings and payback.

Always confirm figures on pmsuryaghar.gov.in and with UPNEDA before you put them in an ad. Never use "free solar panels" wording. Say "subsidy up to ₹78,000" for homes.

Mixing these in one campaign wastes money. A society secretary and a factory owner do not respond to the same headline.

Funnel 1: RWAs and societies in Noida and Greater Noida West

Targeting. Run Meta Ads with tight location targeting around specific societies and sectors. Call out the role in the creative: "RWA committee in Greater Noida West? See what common-area solar can save." The headline does the filtering.

Offer. Lead with a free site survey and a written proposal the committee can forward. Avoid exact savings promises; give a range and the conditions behind it.

Landing page. Send society leads to a page built only for societies, with the GHS/RWA subsidy in two lines (with an "as of" date), a common-area bill savings estimate, and a downloadable proposal.

GBM kit. The sale is closed in the general body meeting. Give the committee a 60-second Hindi or Hinglish explainer video, a one-page PDF and a short FAQ on roof leaks, monsoon output, cleaning and ownership.

We covered this process in detail in our guide to marketing rooftop solar to RWAs in Ghaziabad. The same steps work in Noida; only the DISCOM and the society names change.

Funnel 2: factories, warehouses and commercial buildings

Search first. Factory owners search when a bill shocks them. Bid on terms like "industrial rooftop solar Noida", "solar for factory Greater Noida", "commercial solar installer Noida" and "solar EPC Noida". Use call ads during working hours.

Meta for B2B. Meta can reach business owners by location (industrial sectors) and interests. Use a creative that shows the maths: a sample bill, a generation estimate and a payback range, clearly labelled "illustrative".

Lead form that qualifies. Ask for sanctioned load, average monthly bill, roof type (RCC or metal sheet) and roof area. A plant head who fills this in is a serious lead.

Proposal that answers the CA. Include capex, expected generation, savings range, payback period, structure and warranty terms. Mention that tax benefits such as depreciation may apply and should be confirmed with their chartered accountant. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.

Direct outreach. Use industrial association directories and your existing customers for referrals. A WhatsApp follow-up sequence with a site photo of a completed C&I job (with permission) works better than a generic brochure.

Net metering and DISCOMs in Noida

Noida is largely served by PVVNL, while large parts of Greater Noida are served by NPCL, a private DISCOM. Ask for the bill header on the first call. Net metering, net billing or other options depend on the consumer category and the current UPERC rules, so check the DISCOM's latest process for each project.

Explaining this clearly in your ads and landing pages is a trust signal in itself. Buyers in Noida have often heard conflicting answers from three vendors.

Frequently asked questions

Can factories in Noida get the PM Surya Ghar subsidy?

No. PM Surya Ghar covers residential homes and group housing societies or RWAs. Commercial and industrial rooftops are sold on bill savings and payback, and may have tax benefits such as depreciation, which the buyer should confirm with their chartered accountant.

Which DISCOM handles net metering in Noida and Greater Noida?

Noida is largely served by PVVNL (Paschimanchal Vidyut Vitran Nigam Limited), while large parts of Greater Noida are served by NPCL (Noida Power Company Limited). Check the customer's bill header and follow that DISCOM's current rooftop process.

Does UP give an extra subsidy on top of PM Surya Ghar?

As of November 2026, UP has offered a state top-up for residential rooftop solar of ₹15,000 per kW, capped at ₹30,000. It applies to homes, not factories. Confirm the current status with UPNEDA before using it in any ad.
