PM Surya Ghar: Muft Bijli Yojana has changed how Indian homeowners think about rooftop solar. In August 2026 the Union Energy Minister said that **more than 50 lakh households** had benefited from rooftop solar under the scheme, with over ₹28,000 crore released to beneficiaries by direct benefit transfer. July 2026 alone saw more than 5 lakh homes, the highest monthly number since launch.

For a solar installer, that is both good and bad news. Demand is real, but every homeowner now sees ads from five installers. This guide shows how to use the scheme in your marketing so you get **qualified** leads, not just form fills.

## What homeowners need to understand (and what your ads must say)

Most buyers have heard "free electricity" and "subsidy", but very few understand the details. Your marketing should explain these points in plain Hindi or English:

| Point | What to tell the customer |
|---|---|
| Subsidy amount | 60% of cost up to 2 kW, 40% of the extra cost from 2 to 3 kW, capped at ₹78,000 |
| Rough sizing | 0 to 150 units a month: 1 to 2 kW. 150 to 300 units: 2 to 3 kW. Above 300 units: 3 kW and more |
| Free units | The scheme aims to help homes get up to 300 units a month of free electricity |
| Eligibility | Own house, suitable roof, valid electricity connection, no other rooftop solar subsidy taken |
| Loans | Collateral-free loans at around 7% interest have been reported for systems up to 3 kW |
| Process | Register on the national portal, DISCOM feasibility, install via registered vendor, net metering, inspection, subsidy to bank |

Always point customers to the official portal at pmsuryaghar.gov.in and check the latest numbers before quoting. State-level top-ups also exist in some states, so confirm them for each customer.

## Three campaigns every installer should run

### 1. Google Search for people already looking

People searching "rooftop solar subsidy Ghaziabad" or "3kW solar system price Bhopal" are already close to a decision. Build small, tight ad groups:

- **Subsidy intent:** "PM Surya Ghar vendor near me", "solar subsidy registration help"
- **Price intent:** "3kW solar price", "on-grid solar system cost"
- **Local intent:** "solar company in Gurugram", "solar installer near me"

Add negatives such as *job, salary, franchise, free panel, DIY, wholesale* so you don't pay for the wrong clicks.

### 2. Meta lead or WhatsApp ads to create demand

Instagram and Facebook reach homeowners who haven't searched yet. Lead with **savings**, not panels: "₹2,500 monthly bill? See how much the subsidy covers." Use a short instant form with three qualifying questions:

1. Average monthly electricity bill
2. Do you own the house and roof?
3. Which DISCOM or city?

These three questions remove most time-wasters before your team calls.

### 3. Retargeting with proof

Show people who clicked but didn't enquire your real installations, customer videos and subsidy credit screenshots (with customer permission). Trust is what turns a maybe into a site survey.

## Build a landing page that answers the subsidy question

Your landing page should do three things in the first screen: show the subsidy clearly, ask for the monthly bill, and offer a WhatsApp button. A simple **bill-to-system-size calculator** works very well because it gives the visitor a personal answer and starts a conversation.

Keep it fast on mobile, show your registered-vendor status, add your real installation photos, and include an FAQ that matches what people ask on calls.

## Follow up in minutes, not hours

The biggest leak in solar marketing is slow follow-up. When a homeowner fills three installers' forms, the first one to call usually books the survey. Set up an automatic WhatsApp reply that:

- Thanks them by name
- Shares a short subsidy explainer video
- Asks for a photo of their latest electricity bill
- Offers two survey slots

## Mistakes to avoid

- **Saying "free solar panels".** It attracts the wrong people and can break ad policies. Say "subsidy up to ₹78,000" instead.
- **Looking like a government page.** Don't copy government logos or make it look like an official portal. Be clear that you are a registered vendor.
- **One campaign for every city.** DISCOM processes, state top-ups and buyer language differ between Delhi, Ghaziabad, Gurugram and Bhopal. Split campaigns by area.
- **Measuring cost per lead only.** Track cost per **site survey** and per **installation**. A ₹150 lead that never picks up is more expensive than a ₹400 lead that books.
