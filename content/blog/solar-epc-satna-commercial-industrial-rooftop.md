Satna is one of the most industrial districts in the Vindhya region. Cement plants around Satna and neighbouring Maihar support a long chain of businesses: transporters, crushers, packaging units, workshops, warehouses and traders. Add hospitals, private schools, hotels, cold stores and rice and flour mills, and you have a large pool of buyers with high daytime electricity bills. For a **solar EPC in Satna**, that commercial and industrial (C&I) segment can be worth more than many residential jobs put together.

But C&I buyers do not respond to "subsidy" ads. They buy on payback, reliability and trust. This guide covers how to market to them. Regulatory points are as of November 2026; confirm with official sources before you quote.

## Who buys commercial rooftop solar in Satna

| Buyer | Typical decision-maker | What they care about | Best channel |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mills, crushers, packaging and small factories | Owner or plant head | Bill reduction, payback, downtime | Direct outreach, LinkedIn, referrals |
| Cold stores and warehouses | Owner | Daytime load, payback | Google Search, WhatsApp, associations |
| Hospitals and nursing homes | Director or administrator | Reliable power, savings, reputation | Direct meetings, LinkedIn |

Large cement plants usually handle power through captive plants and big procurement teams, so they are a long shot for a local EPC. The opportunity is in the many mid-sized businesses around them.

## Solar EPC in Satna: what to explain in every pitch

### PM Surya Ghar does not apply to commercial

The PM Surya Ghar subsidy (up to ₹78,000 for a 3 kW home system as of November 2026) is for residential consumers. Do not mention it in commercial ads; it confuses buyers and damages trust. Keep it for your home-solar campaigns. If you also sell to homes, see our [PM Surya Ghar lead guide for installers](/blog/pm-surya-ghar-solar-leads-guide-for-installers/).

### Net metering limits in MP

Satna falls under **MPPKVVCL**, the East Zone DISCOM based in Jabalpur. Under Madhya Pradesh's current net metering regulations, as published by MPERC:

- System size is generally capped at the consumer's **sanctioned load or contract demand**, with a maximum of **500 kW** under net metering.
- The DISCOM checks **distribution transformer capacity** before approving, so feasibility can limit size.
- Larger systems need an HT connection, and surplus units are settled at a low regulated rate at year end, so oversizing rarely pays.
- MPERC issued an amendment to the regulations in 2026. Read the current text on [mperc.in](https://mperc.in) before putting numbers in a proposal.

The practical message for buyers: size the plant to your **daytime consumption**, not your roof area.

### Tax benefits: point to the CA

Many businesses can claim accelerated depreciation on solar plants, which improves payback. The rate and conditions depend on the tax law in force and when the plant is commissioned. Mention it as "possible tax benefit, confirm with your CA" and never promise a figure. This is general information, not tax or financial advice.

## The proposal is your best marketing asset

C&I buyers compare three or four quotes. The EPC that explains the numbers most clearly usually wins. Build every proposal around:

1. **Their last 12 months of bills**, showing daytime units and tariff.
2. **Recommended size** and why, linked to load and roof.
3. **Generation estimate** with a range, not a single promise.
4. **Payback and savings** as a range, with assumptions written down.
5. **Panel and inverter make**, ALMM listing for modules where relevant, and warranties.
6. **Structure design** for Satna's heat, dust and monsoon wind; mention cleaning needs on dusty sites near crushers and highways.
7. **Net metering steps and realistic timelines** with MPPKVVCL.
8. **Payment options**: upfront (CAPEX), bank loans, or third-party RESCO/OPEX models where you or a partner offer them.
9. **O&M plan**: cleaning, monitoring, response time.

Turn this into a short one-page summary and a two-minute video walkthrough the owner can forward to partners or family.

Add negatives for jobs, training, PM Surya Ghar, "free" and residential terms if this campaign is only for commercial. Our [Google Ads guide for solar installers](/blog/google-ads-for-solar-installers-keywords/) has more keyword ideas.

### 2. LinkedIn and direct outreach for decision-makers

Plant heads, hospital administrators and school trustees are reachable on LinkedIn, through local trade associations, chambers of commerce and Rotary or Lions clubs. Share short posts on real projects you have commissioned (with client consent), a simple payback explainer and a monthly generation update from a live plant.

### 3. Site visits and proof

Nothing convinces a factory owner like standing on a working rooftop. Invite prospects to see a commissioned project, show the generation app, and let the existing client speak in their own words. Use only real projects and real figures; never invent case studies.

Meta Ads still help for smaller commercial buyers such as showrooms, hotels and clinics. Our guide to [Meta Ads for solar companies](/blog/meta-ads-for-solar-companies-india/) covers creatives and lead forms.

## Follow-up for long sales cycles

Commercial deals can take one to six months. Keep a simple CRM with stages (enquiry, bill received, site survey, proposal, negotiation, order) and set reminders. Send useful updates on WhatsApp rather than "any update?" messages, and re-engage lost deals before summer, when bills peak.
