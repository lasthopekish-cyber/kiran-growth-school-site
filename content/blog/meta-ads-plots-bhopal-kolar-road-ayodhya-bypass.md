Bhopal's plot market is spread across a few busy corridors. Kolar Road, Ayodhya Bypass, Narmadapuram (Hoshangabad) Road, Misrod and the Bhadbhada and Neelbad side all see plotted colonies and farmhouse projects. Buyers range from salaried families in the city to NRIs and investors from other parts of Madhya Pradesh.

Meta Ads (Facebook and Instagram) are often the biggest lead source for these projects because plot buyers scroll before they search. But plot ads fail when they look like every other "best investment" post. This guide covers what to show, how to target and how to follow up.

## What a Bhopal plot buyer needs to see

Before anyone books a site visit, they want answers to five questions:

1. **Is it legal?** MP RERA registration number, and other approvals such as TNCP layout approval where applicable.
2. **Where exactly is it?** Distance to a landmark they know, not "prime location".
3. **What does it cost?** A starting price per sq ft or a starting plot price.
4. **What is built already?** Roads, boundary wall, electricity, water, drainage, gate.
5. **Who is the developer?** Past projects, office address, real people.

Your creatives should answer at least three of these in the first few seconds.

## RERA basics for plot ads

Under the Real Estate (Regulation and Development) Act, 2016, a registered project's ads must carry the RERA registration number and the authority's website address. Projects that need registration should not be advertised or booked before registration. MP RERA's portal is [rera.mp.gov.in](https://www.rera.mp.gov.in).

Practical rules we follow on every plot campaign:

- Put the MP RERA number on the image or video itself, not only in the caption
- Keep it readable on a phone screen
- Use the same RERA number, price and plot sizes on the landing page and WhatsApp messages
- Avoid "guaranteed returns", "price doubling" or "last few plots" unless it is literally true and documented
- Share approved creatives with channel partners so they don't edit prices or offers

## Targeting by corridor

| Corridor | Who usually shops here | Suggested ad angle |
|---|---|---|
| Kolar Road | Families from Kolar, Awadhpuri, Shahpura, nearby colonies | Ready infrastructure, schools and daily needs nearby |
| Ayodhya Bypass | Buyers from Ayodhya Nagar, Anand Nagar, Piplani, BHEL side | Connectivity, plot sizes for independent houses |
| Narmadapuram Road | Buyers working towards Misrod, Mandideep industrial area | Commute, gated colonies, budget plots |
| City outskirts / farmhouses | Investors, NRIs, weekend-home buyers | Drone views, land size, title clarity |

Treat this as a starting point to test, not a rule. Location data on Meta is approximate, so use a radius around your site plus the residential areas that feed it. Keep audiences broad and let Meta's delivery find buyers; narrow interest stacks rarely work in a city the size of Bhopal.

Run **one campaign per project or corridor**. Mixing a Kolar Road plot and an Ayodhya Bypass plot in the same ad set makes it impossible to tell which one is producing site visits.

## Creatives that work for plots

**Drone reels (15-30 seconds).** Start on a known landmark, then fly to the site. Show internal roads, the gate and the boundary. Add the RERA number and starting price on screen.

**Walkthrough with a person.** A site manager or the developer walking the plot lines in Hindi builds more trust than polished graphics. If shooting every week is hard, an [AI avatar video](/services/ai-avatar-videos/) can explain approvals and payment plans, while the drone footage shows the real site.

**Map carousel.** One card each for distance to the school, hospital, market and main road. Use real, checked distances.

**Price clarity post.** "Plots from ₹X per sq ft, sizes from Y sq ft" with the RERA number. In tier-2 markets, "call for price" often lowers trust.

**Site-visit offer.** Weekend pickup and drop from a known point in the city. This is one of the simplest offers that turns leads into visits.

## Lead forms vs WhatsApp ads

| Option | Good for | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Instant Form (higher intent) | More qualified leads, budget and timeline captured | Fewer leads, slightly higher cost per lead |
| Instant Form (more volume) | Quick volume for launches | Many casual leads, needs fast calling |
| Click-to-WhatsApp | Buyers who prefer chat, easy sharing with family | Needs someone or an automation replying 7 days a week |
| Landing page | Retargeting, Google traffic, detailed info | Must load fast on mobile data |

For most Bhopal plot projects, a mix of a higher-intent form and click-to-WhatsApp works well. Ask two or three qualifying questions: preferred plot size, budget range and when they plan to buy.

## Follow-up: where plot leads are won or lost

Plot buyers often enquire with three or four developers in the same week. The first one to send a useful reply usually gets the site visit.

Follow-up checklist:

- [ ] Reply on WhatsApp within 5 minutes with the location pin, drone video and price sheet
- [ ] Call within the hour during working hours
- [ ] Offer two site-visit slots, ideally on the coming weekend
- [ ] Send a reminder the evening before the visit
- [ ] After the visit, share the layout plan with available plots marked
- [ ] Tag every lead in your CRM by source, corridor and stage

[AI automation](/services/ai-automation/) can handle the first WhatsApp reply, visit reminders and re-engagement messages, so your sales team spends time on calls and visits instead of copy-pasting.

## Measure site visits, not just leads

Cost per lead is easy to see in Ads Manager, but it can mislead. A cheap lead that never visits costs more than a pricier one who books. Track:

- Leads → contacted → site visit booked → site visit done → booking
- Cost per site visit, by corridor and by creative
- Which creatives bring buyers who actually turn up

Results vary with price point, approvals, location and how quickly you follow up, so judge campaigns over a few weeks, not a few days.
